  • Same preprocessing. Use ImageNet mean/std Normalize. Forgetting it is like 🔄 flipped colours (Lab Ex. 3: ≈ 90% → ≈ 30%).
  • Frozen BatchNorm needs eval(). Freezing weights doesn't stop BatchNorm statistics from updating in train() mode (Lesson 8).
  • Small learning rate for the backbone (🔥 shows why), or train the head first, then unfreeze.

🐍 The same thing in PyTorch (Day 2, Notebook 01)

import torch.nn as nn
from torchvision.models import mobilenet_v2, MobileNet_V2_Weights

model = mobilenet_v2(weights=MobileNet_V2_Weights.IMAGENET1K_V1)     # pretrained backbone + old 1000-class head
model.classifier[1] = nn.Linear(1280, 10)                         # new head for 10 classes (random)

# 🔒 feature extraction: freeze the backbone
for p in model.features.parameters():
    p.requires_grad = False
model.features.eval()                                             # keep frozen BatchNorm statistics fixed
opt = torch.optim.Adam(model.classifier.parameters(), lr=3e-3)

# 🔧 fine-tuning: unfreeze and use a SMALL learning rate
for p in model.parameters():
    p.requires_grad = True
opt = torch.optim.Adam(model.parameters(), lr=1e-3)

Here the backbone is a small MLP (100 → 32 → 32) on 10 × 10 pictures; a real backbone is a CNN (Lesson 10). Same three strategies.

🤔 Quick quiz

  1. You have 300 photos of 3 machine parts. 🐣 or 🔒?
    Answer🔒 Reuse a pretrained model. 300 photos is far too few to learn to see from zero.
  2. Why is 🔒 so fast to train?
    AnswerOnly the small head learns (33 knobs here, 12,810 in Day 2). The backbone just runs.
  3. Why did 🔒 lose with 🔄 flipped colours?
    AnswerIts frozen skills look for dark ink. The new photos have light ink, so the skills fire in the wrong places. 🔧 can adjust them.
