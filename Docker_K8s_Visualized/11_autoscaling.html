Autoscaling: more copies when it's busy, fewer when it's quiet

On page 7 you changed replicas. The HorizontalPodAutoscaler (HPA) does it for you: every 15 seconds it looks at the pods' CPU and asks "how many copies would bring the average CPU back to 60%?". Busy → more pods (up to 6). Quiet again → back to 2, after waiting a minute to be sure.

👋 New here? The 30-second version

🧸 Think of it like this

A supermarket opens extra checkouts when the queues get long, and closes them again when it's quiet, but not the very second one queue is empty, in case more customers come.

👆 Do this

  1. Press ▶ Run the load test (16 users for 3 minutes, like k8s/loadtest).
  2. Watch CPU and the number of pods.
  3. Switch off metrics-server and try again.

👀 Notice this

Scaling up is quick; scaling down waits (60 s window). And without metrics-server the HPA is blind: <unknown>, no scaling.

② The rule, and our YAML

  

    

      
desired = ⌈ current pods × current CPU ÷ target CPU ⌉

      
Example: 2 pods at 360% CPU, target 60% → 2 × 360 ÷ 60 = 12 → capped at maxReplicas 6.
      CPU is measured as a % of the pod's request (250m). 360% = 0.9 cores, close to the 1-core limit.

      
Why a 60-second wait before scaling down (stabilizationWindowSeconds)? Traffic is bumpy; without it the HPA would add and remove pods every few seconds ("flapping"). The default is 300 s; we shortened it for class.

    

    
# k8s/06-api-hpa.yaml
apiVersion: autoscaling/v2
kind: HorizontalPodAutoscaler
spec:
  scaleTargetRef: {kind: Deployment, name: cifar-api}
  minReplicas: 2
  maxReplicas: 6
  metrics:
    - type: Resource
      resource:
        name: cpu
        target: {type: Utilization, averageUtilization: 60}
  behavior:
    scaleDown: {stabilizationWindowSeconds: 60}

  1. Run the load test. How many pods at the peak? Does CPU come back under 60%?
      
    Answer6 pods (the maximum). With 16 users the load needs ≈ 12 pods to reach 60%, so even at 6 the CPU stays above target (≈ 120%). maxReplicas protects your wallet and your cluster.
    2. 
    
  2. After the test ends, how long until it's back to 2?
      
    AnswerAbout a minute: the HPA waits until the calculation has said "fewer" for the whole 60-second window, then drops to minReplicas 2.
    3. 
    
  3. Why do small images matter for autoscaling?
      
    AnswerA new pod on a new node must download the image first. 115 MB takes seconds; a 6 GB naive image takes minutes, and the traffic spike may be over by then (page 2).
