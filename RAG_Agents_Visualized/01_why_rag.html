Why RAG? Chat with your own PDF

An LLM learned from the internet. It has never seen your PDF, so when you ask about it, it guesses. RAG fixes this: first we find the right part of the PDF, then we give it to the LLM to answer from.

👋 New here? The 30-second version

🧸 Think of it like this

Closed-book exam: you answer from memory, and when you don't know, you guess. Open-book exam: you look up the right page first, then answer. RAG turns the LLM's closed-book exam into an open-book exam.

👀 Notice this

With RAG the answer is correct and shows where it came from (the page), so you can check it.

📖 Words, made easy:
LLM = the model that writes answers (we use Llama 3.3 on Groq) RAG = Retrieval-Augmented Generation: find, then answerRetrieve = find and fetch Source = the page the answer came from

🧪 Try this (guess first!)

  1. Why does the "LLM alone" answer sound so confident even when it's wrong?
    AnswerAn LLM always writes fluent text. It does not know that it doesn't know. It gives a "typical company" answer, not Acme's answer.
  2. Could we just retrain the LLM on our PDF instead?
    AnswerThat is slow and costly, and you'd redo it every time the PDF changes. With RAG you just swap the PDF.
  3. Why is the "(page 1)" source useful?
    AnswerYou can open the PDF and check the answer yourself. You don't have to just trust the model.
