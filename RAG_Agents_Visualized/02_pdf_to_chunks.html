PDF → text → chunks

First we read the text out of the PDF (with pypdf). Then we cut it into small pieces, called chunks. Each piece is about 500 characters, and neighbours share 100 characters (the overlap).

👋 New here? The 30-second version

🧸 Think of it like this

A 300-page book is too much to read for one question. So you cut it into index cards, one small topic per card. To answer a question, you only pick up the few cards that matter.

👆 Do this

  1. In ①, move the chunk size slider.
  2. Watch how many chunks you get.
  3. In ②, switch overlap off and on.

👀 Notice this

Small chunks = more cards, each about one thing. The yellow part is repeated from the card before.

📖 Words, made easy:
Chunk = one small piece of the textChunk size = how many characters in each piece Overlap = the bit each piece repeats from the one beforeCharacter = one letter, digit, space or comma

① Cut the handbook into chunks

Why cut at all? The LLM can't read 300 pages for every question, and a small piece about one topic is much easier to find.

② Why the overlap?

Question: "How much unused leave can I carry forward?"

🧪 Try this (guess first!)

  1. Move the slider to 200. Then to 800. What happens to the number of chunks?
    AnswerSmaller chunks give more pieces. Bigger chunks give fewer pieces, but each one mixes several topics.
  2. Why not make one giant chunk of the whole PDF?
    AnswerThen every search returns "everything". The LLM gets lots of text that has nothing to do with the question. Small pieces keep the answer precise.
  3. Why not make chunks of just 20 characters?
    AnswerA piece that small can't hold a full idea, like "18 days of annual leave per year". About 500 characters is a good middle.
