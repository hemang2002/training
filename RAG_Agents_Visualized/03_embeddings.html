Embeddings: turning meaning into numbers

We have small chunks of the handbook. Now we need to find the right chunk for a question. An embedding model turns each chunk into a list of numbers. Chunks that mean similar things get similar numbers, so they sit close together.

👋 New here? The 30-second version

🧸 Think of it like this

A library that shelves books by meaning, not by title. All the cooking books stand together, all the travel books stand together. To find an answer, you walk to the right shelf and grab the nearest books.

👆 Do this

  1. Hover or tap the dots in ① to read each chunk.
  2. Press a question button. A pin drops on the map.
  3. See which 3 chunks are closest.

👀 Notice this

The question "How many leave days?" lands next to the leave chunks, even though it does not use the same words.

📖 Words, made easy:
Embedding = a list of numbers that stands for the meaning of a text Embedding model = the program that makes those numbers (we use all-MiniLM-L6-v2, free, on your laptop) Similarity score = how close two meanings are, from 0 (not related) to 1 (same meaning)

The real embedding has 384 numbers per chunk. Nobody can draw 384 directions, so we squash it down to 2 to put it on a flat map. The idea stays the same: close = similar meaning.

② One sentence becomes 384 numbers

"Annual leave is 18 days per year."→ embedding model →
[0.12, -0.05, 0.33, 0.08, -0.21, … 384 numbers]

One number alone means nothing to us. What matters: similar sentences get similar numbers. Compare the question with two chunks:

❓ How many leave days?vsAnnual leave is 18 days0.71 close
❓ How many leave days?vsLaptop replaced every 3 years0.08 far

The score tells how much the two lists of numbers "point the same way". Near 1 = same meaning. Near 0 = not related.

🧪 Try this (guess first!)

  1. Pick "How much money for travel?". Why is the laptop chunk on the list at all?
    AnswerWe always take the top 3. The laptop chunk is only third, with a low score. It is in the same "money & equipment" area, but it is not really about travel.
  2. The question "Can I work from home on Friday?" has no word "week" in it. How does it still find "up to 2 days per week"?
    AnswerEmbeddings match meaning, not exact words. "Friday" and "per week" are both about days you work, so the numbers end up close.
  3. Does the embedding model need the internet or a paid key?
    AnswerNo. all-MiniLM-L6-v2 is open-source and runs on your own laptop. Only the final answer (page 4) uses the Groq API.
